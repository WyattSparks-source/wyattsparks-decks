Market read by John Wyatt, for Antonio Colicchio · October 2026

SmashQueue solves a real problem. Others have noticed it too.

A growing field of tools already manages who plays next at pickleball venues, so the case is narrow. This read ends with a small test to find out if it holds.

Start here

SmashQueue replaces the paddle rack with a line on a phone.

The problem

Who plays next?

At open play, anyone can show up and join a game. Players set their paddles in a rack to hold a place in line. On busy nights the line gets confusing, and venues put staff or a volunteer on it.

The product

SmashQueue

Players scan a code to join the line, with no app to download. SmashQueue assigns the courts and tells players when they are up. Antonio built it and has shown it to fewer than 10 Columbus venues.

This deck

One question

Is there a business here worth John's and Antonio's time? This is John's read of the market, written so John and Antonio can decide the next step together.

The hurdle

$480K a year is the line. That is 400 clubs paying $100 a month.

That is the revenue where this becomes worth two partners' time, and it takes 11% to 22% of likely buyers. It has to be reached with one sales hire at most and support light enough to outsource. If the math can't clear it, it's not worth going further.

$100 a month is a working price. Venues told Antonio that hundreds a month is possible and thousands is not. That leaves about $960 to win each club, which is one year of fees ($1,200) less an estimated 20% to run the service.

Who is already there

Three tools are furthest along.

Free and fast

PickleQ

Launched January 2026. Free up to 4 courts, and venues sign up on their own.

16,000+ "clubs" by its own count, where a club can be any group of players. Feeds DUPR, a widely used player rating system.

Funded, for large clubs

PodPlay

$8M raised. 200+ locations by its own count, sold with screens and cameras installed on court.

Exclusive deal with Pickleball Kingdom, a franchise chain.

One developer

PaddleStack

Free for players, and venues sign up on their own.

11 app ratings and 100+ installs. No funding or chain ties found.

Other queue tools exist and more are being built. Prices, funding and team sizes are in the appendix.

What is left

Two openings remain.

On venue fees alone, 400 clubs looks out of reach without someone else's sales channel. That is why the second opening matters most.

Already served

Small groups and PodPlay clubs

PickleQ is free up to 4 courts, so the smallest organizers have no reason to pay. Above 4 courts it charges. PodPlay's clubs already have a queue built in.

Opening one

Venues with no booking software

Independent clubs and paid rec centers with 5 or more courts. PickleQ's paid tier and newer tools aim here too.

Opening two

Clubs on CourtReserve

The largest booking platform found has no queue feature that turned up in this research.

How many venues sit in each opening is not known. The competitors' counts are worldwide and include tennis, so they can't be subtracted from the US numbers.

The CourtReserve question

The largest platform found has no "who's next" feature.

01

What it is

CourtReserve runs booking and membership for 2,300+ facilities worldwide. Clubs list open play there as an event. On the night itself, at least one of its clubs still uses a paddle rack.

02

The opening

A club can switch on a data connection (called an API) that lets an outside tool see who signed up. Other tools already use it. CourtReserve also lists 20 outside tools it recommends to its clubs, and none of those manages the line.

03

The limits

The connection is only on CourtReserve's $369-a-month plan and up, so its smallest clubs are out. Its partner terms are unpublished, and it could build the feature itself.

Why CourtReserve has not built this is unknown, and how much the connection shares is unconfirmed. Both get answered inside a pilot.

Why venues take the demo

Venues liked the demo, yet didn't seem to know the other tools exist.

01

Thousands of problems

Businesses and municipalities have thousands of problems. When one is brought to their attention with a potential solution, they are usually happy to talk about it.

02

Not yet a top problem

For their biggest problems, they have already searched for solutions. Not knowing the other tools out there suggests this is not one of them.

03

One more hurdle

Not knowing the other solutions today doesn't mean they won't compare when it is time to decide. That adds one more hurdle to each sale.

This is why the next step tests payment and not interest.

Risks

Service on the night is the biggest risk.

01

Service and on-call

If many open-play nights fall on Friday evenings, someone has to be reachable. Handholding, setup and being on call for venues is John's weakest link, and Antonio will often be traveling. John can spend 10 Friday evenings on site during pilots. Beyond that it needs to be outsourced.

02

No proven edge yet

Above 4 courts PickleQ charges too, at a price it doesn't publish, and other free tools exist. What SmashQueue does better has to show up in the pilots.

03

Platform dependence

Building on CourtReserve ties SmashQueue to a company that could add the queue itself.

Sales, demos, list building, sales operations and urgent software fixes are covered on John's side.

The 90-day test

Three steps show whether venues pay and whether CourtReserve is a way in.

Step 1

Two paid pilots

Two Columbus venues pay about $100 a month from day one. One of the two already uses CourtReserve on its $369 plan or higher. Venues have shown interest, and the pilot tests whether they pay.

Step 2

Connect to CourtReserve

That club switches on the data connection in its CourtReserve settings. Players who sign up for open play there then appear in the SmashQueue line, with no double entry.

Step 3

Apply as a partner

With a working connection and a club willing to vouch for it, apply to become the tool CourtReserve recommends for managing the line.

Two pilots do not prove 400 clubs. They show whether anyone pays and whether the CourtReserve route works. Proposed pass mark: both still paying at 90 days, and the connection working at one.

The decision

Run a 90-day test with two paid pilots in Columbus.

This is still an exploratory conversation. The cost is time, with no outside money: about 8 hours a week from John, including 10 Friday evenings on site. If the test passes, the next step is a plan to reach 400 clubs. If not, it stops there.
