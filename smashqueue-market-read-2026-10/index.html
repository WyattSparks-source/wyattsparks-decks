A growing field of tools already runs open-play queues, and three of them have made the most progress.

The hurdle

$480K a year is the line: 400 clubs paying $100 a month.

That is 11% to 22% of likely buyers, reached with one sales hire at most and no service staff. If I can't write a strategy that clears it, the idea stops here.

400 clubs x $100 x 12 months. At $100 a month, a 12-month payback leaves about $950 to win each club (my estimate).

Who is already there

Three tools already run the queue.

Free and fast

PickleQ

  • Launched January 2026
  • 16,000+ clubs created, worldwide, self-reported
  • Free to 4 courts. Paid tier price unpublished
  • Self-serve signup, no hardware
  • Reports matches into DUPR
  • Funding and team: none found

Funded and upmarket

PodPlay

  • In market since at least 2023
  • 200+ locations, 1M users
  • $30 to $350 per court a month (third-party figure)
  • Sold with court hardware
  • Exclusive with Pickleball Kingdom
  • $8M raised. Team size unpublished

One developer

PaddleStack

  • Launch date unclear
  • 11 App Store ratings, 100+ Android installs
  • Free for players. Venue price unpublished
  • Self-serve, no hardware
  • No league or rating ties found
  • No funding found. Team of one

What is left

The open space is venues with 5+ courts and no club software.

01

Clubs on a platform

Most paying clubs already run CourtReserve, PodPlay or Playbypoint. A queue is one release away for each of them, and PodPlay has shipped it.

02

Small groups

PickleQ is free up to 4 courts, so the smallest organizers have no reason to pay.

03

What is open

Rec centers, municipal complexes and independents with 5+ courts. More builders are arriving here too: SliceWin, Racketa and Court10 show up on Reddit, unverified.

Platform counts are worldwide and include tennis, so they do not subtract cleanly from the US buyer pool.

Why venues said yes anyway

Interest in a demo is not the same as a search for a fix.

01

The rack is free

A paddle rack costs under $50 and players enforce it themselves. One Oregon parks board saw no pattern of complaints.

02

Nobody is shopping

Players on Reddit describe a rack, a 12-minute timer, or "one dude at our local court moving things along." They also resent apps that move features behind a paywall.

03

A yes costs nothing

About ten venues were interested. As far as I know, none has been asked to pay yet.

What happens now

Run a 90-day test before anyone commits more.

One conversation with CourtReserve's partner program, and two Columbus pilots asked to pay. If neither moves, we have the answer and have spent very little.
