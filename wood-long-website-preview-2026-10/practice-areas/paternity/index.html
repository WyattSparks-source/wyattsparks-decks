Paternity

When parents are not married, Ohio law does not recognize the father's legal relationship to a child until paternity is established under ORC Chapter 3111. That can happen through a signed acknowledgment, a CSEA genetic-testing order, or a juvenile court case. Paternity creates support duties, but custody and parenting time require separate court orders.

Updated October 9, 2026.

What does establishing paternity mean in Ohio?

Establishing paternity means creating a legal finding that a man is a child’s father. If the parents are married when the child is born, Ohio presumes the husband is the father under ORC 3111.03. If the parents are not married, there is no legal father until paternity is established under ORC Chapter 3111, even if everyone agrees who the father is.

Paternity matters for the child, the mother and the father. It makes child support possible, gives the child inheritance rights and access to benefits through the father, and gives the father the legal standing to ask a court for custody or parenting time.

What are the ways to establish paternity in Ohio?

There are three main paths.

1. Acknowledgment of paternity. Both parents can sign an acknowledgment of paternity affidavit, often at the hospital after the birth or later through the county child support enforcement agency (CSEA) or a local health department. Both parents must sign the form, and it becomes part of the state’s birth registry records.

2. Administrative genetic testing through the CSEA. Either parent can ask the CSEA to determine paternity. The agency schedules genetic testing, usually a cheek swab, and then issues an administrative order finding that a father-child relationship does or does not exist. A party who disagrees can file an action in juvenile court within a short deadline after the order.

3. A parentage action in juvenile court. A mother, alleged father, child or the CSEA can file a parentage case in juvenile court. The court can order genetic testing, decide paternity, and in the same case address child support and, if requested, custody and parenting time. A parentage action can generally be filed until the child’s 23rd birthday under ORC 3111.05.

Can an acknowledgment of paternity be undone?

Only within tight limits. Under ORC 3111.27, either signer can rescind the acknowledgment within 60 days of the most recent signature by requesting a genetic-testing determination through the CSEA and giving the required notice. After 60 days, the acknowledgment becomes final.

Once final, it can only be challenged in court under ORC 3111.28 on the grounds of fraud, duress or material mistake of fact, and that action must be filed within one year after the acknowledgment became final. If there is any doubt about who the father is, genetic testing before signing is the safer choice.

What does paternity give a father, and what doesn’t it?

Establishing paternity gives a father:

  • the legal status of father, including being listed on the birth certificate
  • the right to file in juvenile court for custody, shared parenting or parenting time
  • standing in later cases involving the child, such as a proposed stepparent adoption

It also brings a duty to support the child. Once paternity is established, a child support order can be set, either administratively or by the court.

What paternity does not do is give the father custody or a parenting schedule. Under ORC 3109.042, an unmarried mother is the sole residential parent and legal custodian until a court issues an order allocating parental rights and responsibilities. A father who wants time with his child needs a separate juvenile court order. In that case, the court treats both parents as equals and decides based on the child’s best interest. See our child custody page.

Which is better: the CSEA or juvenile court?

It depends on what you need. The CSEA process is usually simpler and lower cost if the only questions are who the father is and how much support is owed. Juvenile court makes more sense if you also need custody, parenting time or a shared parenting plan, if paternity is disputed in a complicated way, or if you need to challenge an acknowledgment or an administrative order.

Paternity can also matter to extended family. Paternal grandparents and other relatives of the father generally cannot seek visitation with a child of unmarried parents until paternity is established. See our grandparent rights page.

Common questions

Does signing the birth certificate make me the legal father in Ohio?

Signing an acknowledgment of paternity affidavit, which is often offered at the hospital, legally establishes paternity once it becomes final. Either signer can rescind it within 60 days of the last signature by requesting a genetic-testing determination through the child support agency. After that, it can only be challenged in court for fraud, duress or material mistake of fact, within one year.

If paternity is established, do I automatically get parenting time?

No. Under ORC 3109.042, an unmarried mother remains the sole residential parent and legal custodian until a court orders otherwise, even after paternity is established. A father must file in juvenile court for an order on custody, shared parenting or parenting time. Once he files, the court treats both parents equally and decides based on the child's best interest.

How do I get a DNA test to establish paternity in Ohio?

You can ask the county child support enforcement agency to arrange genetic testing, usually by cheek swab. The agency then issues an administrative order finding that a father-child relationship does or does not exist. You can also file a parentage action in juvenile court, and the court can order genetic testing as part of that case.

Is there a deadline to establish paternity in Ohio?

A parentage action under ORC Chapter 3111 can generally be brought until five years after the child turns 18, meaning up to the child's 23rd birthday. Child support issues, inheritance rights and the father's ability to seek parenting time all depend on paternity, so most families are better served by establishing it early.
