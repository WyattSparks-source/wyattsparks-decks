Dissolution

A dissolution ends an Ohio marriage when both spouses agree on every issue before filing. You file a joint petition with a signed separation agreement covering property, debts, support and parenting, and the court holds a hearing 30 to 90 days later. If both spouses confirm under oath that they still agree, the court grants the dissolution.

Updated October 9, 2026.

What is a dissolution of marriage in Ohio?

A dissolution is a way to end a marriage when you and your spouse have already agreed on everything. It is governed by ORC 3105.61 through 3105.65. There is no complaint against the other spouse and no need to prove grounds. You file together, as co-petitioners.

In Columbus, a dissolution is filed with the Franklin County Court of Common Pleas, Division of Domestic Relations and Juvenile Branch. At least one spouse must have been an Ohio resident for six months immediately before filing, under ORC 3105.62.

What goes into the separation agreement?

The separation agreement is the core of a dissolution, and it must settle every issue in your marriage. Under ORC 3105.63, both spouses sign it and it is filed with the petition. It must cover:

  • All property and debts, including the home, vehicles, bank and retirement accounts, and credit cards. See property division.
  • Spousal support, including whether any will be paid, how much, for how long, and whether the court keeps the power to change it later. See spousal support.
  • Parenting, if you have minor children: a shared parenting plan or an allocation of parental rights, a parenting schedule, and child support.

The court reviews the agreement at the hearing. If you leave an issue out, it may not be possible to come back for it later, so completeness matters.

How does the dissolution hearing work?

The court must hold the hearing at least 30 days and no more than 90 days after the petition is filed, as required by ORC 3105.64. Both spouses must attend. Each of you will be asked, under oath, whether you signed the separation agreement voluntarily, whether you are satisfied with it, and whether you want the marriage dissolved.

If the court approves the agreement and both spouses confirm it, the court grants a decree of dissolution under ORC 3105.65. The separation agreement becomes part of that decree and is enforceable like any court order. If either spouse withdraws consent before the decree, the court will not grant it.

What is the difference between divorce and dissolution?

Both end the marriage and lead to the same result: a court order dividing property and setting support and parenting terms. The difference is how you get there.

Dissolution Divorce
Who files Both spouses, together One spouse files against the other
Grounds needed No Yes, under ORC 3105.01
Agreement required Full agreement before filing No; the court decides open issues
Timeline after filing Hearing 30–90 days after filing Depends on how contested the case is
Trial No Possible, if issues do not settle

A dissolution is often faster, less expensive and more private, but it only works if you truly agree on every issue. If you do not, a divorce gives the court the power to decide.

Can a dissolution be converted to a divorce, or a divorce to a dissolution?

Yes, in both directions. Under ORC 3105.08, spouses in a pending divorce can convert it to a dissolution at any time before a final decree, by filing a motion and the documents a dissolution requires.

Going the other way, if a dissolution falls apart before the hearing, either spouse may ask the court to convert the dissolution to a divorce, rather than starting a new case.

Common questions

How long does a dissolution take in Ohio?

Once the petition is filed, Ohio law requires the court to hold the final hearing no sooner than 30 days and no later than 90 days after filing, under ORC 3105.64. The time before filing varies more. Reaching a full separation agreement and preparing the paperwork can take a few weeks or several months, depending on your finances, your children and how quickly you and your spouse agree.

Do we both have to go to the dissolution hearing?

Yes. Ohio law requires both spouses to appear at the final hearing. Each spouse tells the court, under oath, that they voluntarily signed the separation agreement, are satisfied with it, and want the marriage dissolved. If one spouse does not appear or no longer agrees, the court cannot grant the dissolution on that petition.

Can we use one lawyer for a dissolution?

A lawyer can represent only one spouse. The other spouse can hire their own lawyer or go without one. Even when you agree on everything, each spouse has separate interests in the property, support and parenting terms, so having your own lawyer review the separation agreement before you sign it helps you understand what you are agreeing to.

What if we start a divorce and then reach an agreement?

You can ask to convert the divorce to a dissolution. Under ORC 3105.08, at any time before a final divorce decree, the spouses may file a motion to convert the case and file the documents a dissolution requires, including a signed separation agreement. The court then treats the case as a dissolution and sets the hearing.
