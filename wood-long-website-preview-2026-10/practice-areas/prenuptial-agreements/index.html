Prenuptial Agreements

A prenuptial agreement is a written contract two people sign before marriage that sets how property, debts and spousal support will be handled if the marriage ends. Since 2023, Ohio also allows married couples to sign postnuptial agreements. Either kind must be entered freely, with full financial disclosure, and without fraud, duress or overreaching to be enforced.

Updated October 9, 2026.

What is a prenuptial agreement?

A prenuptial (or antenuptial) agreement is a contract two people sign before marriage that sets what happens to their property, debts and, often, spousal support if the marriage ends by divorce or death. Without one, Ohio’s default rules on property division and spousal support apply.

Ohio recognizes these agreements. Property excluded by a valid prenuptial agreement is treated as separate property in a divorce under ORC 3105.171. Because it is an agreement made in consideration of marriage, it must be in writing and signed under Ohio’s statute of frauds, ORC 1335.05.

What can a prenup cover in Ohio?

A prenuptial agreement can address:

  • Which property stays separate, such as a home, a business, savings or an expected inheritance
  • How property acquired during the marriage will be divided
  • How debts will be handled, including student loans or business debt
  • Whether spousal support will be paid, how much, or that it will be waived
  • What each spouse receives at death, in coordination with wills and estate plans

A prenup cannot decide child custody, parenting time or child support. Those are decided based on the children’s interests when the issue comes up.

What makes a prenup enforceable in Ohio?

Ohio courts generally enforce a prenuptial agreement if three things are true:

  1. It was entered into freely, without fraud, duress, coercion or overreaching.
  2. There was full disclosure, or each person had full knowledge and understanding of the nature, value and extent of the other’s property.
  3. The terms do not promote or encourage divorce or let one person profit from ending the marriage.

Spousal support provisions get an added check. Even if the agreement was valid when signed, a court may refuse to enforce a support provision that has become unconscionable at the time of divorce because circumstances changed in ways the couple did not foresee, such as a serious illness.

An agreement does not have to split things evenly to be valid. But a one-sided agreement draws closer scrutiny of how it was signed and what each person knew.

Why does timing matter?

Timing is one of the main ways a prenup is challenged. Ohio sets no deadline, but an agreement presented days before the wedding, after invitations are out and plans are fixed, raises questions about whether the signature was truly voluntary. Courts have looked at whether the person had a real opportunity to consult a lawyer and enough time to review the terms.

Practical steps that help: start several months before the wedding, exchange written financial disclosures (attached to the agreement), and have each person represented by their own lawyer.

Can married couples sign a postnuptial agreement in Ohio?

Yes. For decades Ohio law barred spouses from changing their legal relations by contract during the marriage, except by agreeing to an immediate separation. In 2023, Ohio amended ORC 3103.06 and added ORC 3103.061 to permit postnuptial agreements.

Under the statute, a postnuptial agreement must be in writing and signed by both spouses; entered into freely without fraud, duress, coercion or overreaching; made with full disclosure, or full knowledge and understanding, of the nature, value and extent of the other spouse’s property, income and liabilities; and have terms that are not unconscionable.

Couples use postnuptial agreements after an inheritance, when one spouse starts or buys a business, after one spouse leaves work to care for children, or to rebuild trust after a hard period. A postnup can also update or replace a prenup signed before the marriage.

Common questions

Are prenuptial agreements enforceable in Ohio?

Yes, if they meet the standards Ohio courts apply. The agreement must be in writing and signed, entered into freely without fraud, duress, coercion or overreaching, and made with full disclosure or full knowledge of the other person's assets. Its terms must not promote divorce. Spousal support terms can also be reviewed at the time of divorce to see whether they have become unconscionable because circumstances changed.

Can we sign a postnuptial agreement in Ohio?

Yes. Before 2023, Ohio law did not allow spouses to change their legal relations by contract during the marriage, except to agree to an immediate separation. An amendment to ORC 3103.06 and the new ORC 3103.061 now permit postnuptial agreements. They must be in writing, signed by both spouses, entered freely, based on full disclosure, and not unconscionable.

How far before the wedding should we sign a prenup?

Ohio law sets no fixed deadline, but earlier is better. When an agreement is presented shortly before the wedding, a court may look more closely at whether it was signed freely, especially if one person had no real chance to consult a lawyer. Starting the conversation and exchanging financial information a few months ahead gives both people time to review and negotiate.

Can a prenup decide child custody or child support?

No. Ohio courts decide custody, parenting time and child support based on the children's interests at the time of the divorce, and parents cannot give up a child's right to support in advance. A prenuptial or postnuptial agreement can address property, debts and spousal support, but any terms about children are not binding on the court.
