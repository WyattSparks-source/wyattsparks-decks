Spousal Support

Spousal support, often called alimony, is money one spouse pays the other during or after an Ohio divorce or legal separation. Ohio has no statewide formula. The court decides whether support is appropriate and reasonable, and how much and how long, by weighing the factors in ORC 3105.18, and it can change the award later only if the decree keeps that power.

Updated October 9, 2026.

What is spousal support in Ohio?

Spousal support is a payment from one spouse to the other, set by the court or agreed by the spouses, for sustenance and support. Ohio’s statute, ORC 3105.18, uses the term “spousal support” rather than alimony. Support can be paid in installments, as a lump sum, or both.

Spousal support is decided after property is divided. The court first divides marital property under the property division rules, then looks at whether support is still needed to make the outcome appropriate and reasonable.

How do Ohio courts decide spousal support?

The court decides whether support is appropriate and reasonable, and sets its amount, terms and length, by considering the factors in ORC 3105.18(C)(1). They include:

  • Each spouse’s income from all sources, including income from property divided in the divorce
  • Each spouse’s earning ability
  • Each spouse’s age and physical, mental and emotional condition
  • Retirement benefits of each spouse
  • How long the marriage lasted
  • Whether it would be inappropriate for a spouse to work outside the home because they will be caring for a minor child
  • The standard of living during the marriage
  • Each spouse’s education
  • Each spouse’s assets and debts, including court-ordered payments
  • Contributions one spouse made to the other’s education, training or earning ability
  • The time and expense a spouse would need to get education or training to find suitable work, if they intend to
  • Tax consequences of a support award
  • Lost income-production capacity caused by a spouse’s marital responsibilities
  • Any other factor the court finds relevant

Fault in the breakdown of the marriage is not one of the listed factors.

Is there a formula for spousal support in Ohio?

No, Ohio has no statewide formula. Each case turns on its facts and the judge or magistrate’s weighing of the statutory factors. That makes outcomes harder to predict than child support, and it is one reason careful preparation of income and expense information matters.

How long does spousal support last?

The court sets the length of support based on the same factors, and the length of the marriage carries significant weight. A short marriage may result in short-term support or none. A longer marriage, or a spouse who left the workforce for many years, may lead to longer support. In some long marriages, the court may order support with no set end date, particularly when one spouse is unlikely to become self-supporting.

Under ORC 3105.18(B), spousal support ends when either spouse dies, unless the order says otherwise. Decrees and agreements often also list other events that end support, such as the receiving spouse remarrying or living with a new partner.

Can spousal support be modified?

Spousal support can be modified only if the court retained jurisdiction to do so. Under ORC 3105.18(E), the divorce decree (or, in a dissolution, the separation agreement) must expressly allow the court to change the amount or terms. If it does not, the award generally cannot be changed later.

When jurisdiction is retained, the spouse asking for a change must show a change in circumstances under ORC 3105.18(F). That includes things like an involuntary drop in income, a significant raise, or an increase or decrease in living expenses. The change must be substantial enough that the existing award is no longer reasonable and appropriate, and it must not have been taken into account when support was first set. Whether to retain jurisdiction is one of the most important choices in a support agreement. For more, see post-decree modification.

What is temporary spousal support?

Temporary spousal support is support ordered while a divorce or legal separation is pending. ORC 3105.18 allows the court to award reasonable temporary support during the case. It is usually decided early, based on financial affidavits, pay records and household budgets. It is meant to keep both households stable until the final decree and does not decide the final award.

Common questions

Is there a spousal support calculator in Ohio?

No. Unlike child support, Ohio has no statewide formula for spousal support. The court weighs the factors in ORC 3105.18, such as each spouse's income and earning ability, the length of the marriage and the standard of living, and decides what is appropriate and reasonable. Online calculators or rules of thumb you may see are not Ohio law and can be misleading for your situation.

How long does spousal support last in Ohio?

There is no fixed rule. The court sets a duration based on the ORC 3105.18 factors, and the length of the marriage weighs heavily. Shorter marriages often lead to shorter support, or none. In long marriages, or where one spouse has little ability to become self-supporting, support may last longer or have no set end date. Support ends when either spouse dies unless the order says otherwise.

Can spousal support be changed after the divorce?

Only if the decree allows it. Under ORC 3105.18(E), the court can modify spousal support only when the divorce decree or the dissolution separation agreement expressly keeps the court's power to do so. Even then, the person asking must show a substantial change in circumstances that was not taken into account when support was first set, such as a significant change in income.

Can I get support while the divorce is pending?

Yes. Under ORC 3105.18, the court can order reasonable temporary spousal support while a divorce or legal separation case is open. Temporary support helps cover living expenses until the final decree. It is decided early in the case, usually from income and expense information both spouses submit, and it does not lock in what the final award will be.
