Your first steps

What to expect

Here is how working with us starts, from the first call to a plan for your case.

1. Contact us

Call (614) 567-3031 or send the consultation request form. We will ask for your name, the other party’s name and a short description of your situation. We use the other party’s name only to check for conflicts of interest: we cannot advise you if we already represent or have consulted with the other side.

2. Your consultation

We set aside 90 minutes, in person at our office, by Zoom or by phone. You describe what has happened and what you want. We explain how Ohio law applies to your situation, the options realistically open to you, the likely steps and timeline, and what the work is likely to cost.

There is a fee for the consultation. If you hire us, it is credited toward your fees. See how our fees work.

3. A plan for your case

If you decide to hire us, we sign an engagement agreement that sets out the scope of the work and how billing works. Then we build a plan: what needs to be filed or negotiated first, what information we need from you, and what you can expect in the coming weeks.

4. While your case is open

You will know who is working on your case and how to reach them. We keep you informed at each stage, explain each decision before you make it, and tell you plainly when something is or isn’t likely to work.

Common questions

What should I bring to my consultation?

You do not have to bring anything. It helps to have a general idea of your assets and debts if you are separating from a spouse, and to bring any court papers you have been served with or any current court orders. Financial documents are welcome but not required.

Do I have to meet in person?

No. We prefer to meet at our Columbus office, but we also hold consultations by Zoom or by phone if that works better for you.

Can I bring my children?

We ask that you arrange childcare. Keeping children away from the legal process protects them, and it lets you speak freely during the consultation.

I already have a lawyer. Can I get a second opinion?

Yes. A second opinion can help you understand your options and feel confident in your representation. Tell us about your current attorney when you contact us so we can check for conflicts.
